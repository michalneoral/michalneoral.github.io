Histogram

The histogram as a density estimate

The true density \(\cp(\cx)\) exists, but we do not know it.

We only get \(N = 12\) samples drawn from it: \(\cx_i \sim \cp(\cx)\).

Bins of width \(w_b\), \(N_b\) samples in bin \(b\). What is the height of the bin?

Compared with the true \(\cp(\cx)\): one bin in a dense region is empty.

Derivation: the height of a bin

The bin contains \(N_b\) of the \(N\) samples: \[ P(\cx \in \text{bin } b) \approx \frac{N_b}{N} \]

Density is probability per unit length: \[ \cph_b = \frac{N_b}{N \cdot w_b} \]

The estimate integrates to one: \[ \sum_b \cph_b \, w_b = \sum_b \frac{N_b}{N} = 1 \]

Here: \(\cph_b = \frac{5}{12 \cdot 1.5} = 0.28\) for the highest bin.

\(\cph_b\) is a height, not a probability: 6 of 12 samples in a bin of width 0.25 give \(\cph_b = 2\)

Histogram: pros and cons

Pros

  • Simple, easy to visualise
  • We do not need to keep the samples \(\cx_i\)

Cons

  • Step-like, bins can be empty
  • How to choose \(w_b\)?
  • \(D > 2\): \(B^D\) bins, mostly empty

Towards Parzen windows

More dimensions: \(\;\cph(\cx) = \frac{K}{N V}\)

The histogram in \(D\) dimensions: \(K\) samples in a volume \(V\) around \(\cx\).

Fix \(K\), find the smallest \(V\) containing \(K\) samples: \(k\)-nearest neighbours.

Fix \(V\), count the samples \(K\) inside: Parzen windows.

The volume can be a ball or a hypercube: the cube is easy to count in.

Counting the samples in a window

Window function: the unit hypercube centred at the origin \[ \varphi(\mathbf{u}) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } |u^j| \le \tfrac{1}{2} \text{ for all } j = 1, \dots, D \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

\(\varphi\big(\frac{\cx - \cx_i}{\ch}\big) = 1\) iff \(\cx_i\) lies in the hypercube of side \(\ch\) around \(\cx\), so \(V = \ch^D\) and \[ K = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \varphi\Big(\frac{\cx - \cx_i}{\ch}\Big) \]

\[ \cph_\ch(\cx) = \frac{K}{N V} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \underbrace{\frac{1}{\ch^D}\, \varphi\Big(\frac{\cx - \cx_i}{\ch}\Big)}_{K_\ch(\cx, \cx_i)} \]

A sum of kernels

\(N = 6\) samples \(\cx_i\).

Around every sample a box of width \(\ch\) and height \(\frac{1}{N\ch}\) (area \(\frac{1}{N}\)).

\(\cph_\ch(\cx)\) is their sum: still step-like, but no fixed bins.

A Gaussian kernel around every sample, again with area \(\frac{1}{N}\).

Their sum is a smooth estimate.

Kernels \(K_\text{type}(\cx, \cx_i)\)

\[ K_\text{box}(\cx, \cx_i) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{\ch} & \text{if } |\cx - \cx_i| \le \frac{\ch}{2} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

\[ \int K_\text{box}(\cx, \cx_i)\, d\cx = \ch \cdot \frac{1}{\ch} = 1 \]

\[ K_\text{tri}(\cx, \cx_i) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{\ch} \Big(1 - \frac{|\cx - \cx_i|}{\ch}\Big) & \text{if } |\cx - \cx_i| \le \ch \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

\[ \int K_\text{tri}(\cx, \cx_i)\, d\cx = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\ch \cdot \frac{1}{\ch} = 1 \]

\[ K_\text{Gauss}(\cx, \cx_i) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\, \ch}\, e^{-\frac{(\cx - \cx_i)^2}{2\ch^2}} \]

\[ \int K_\text{Gauss}(\cx, \cx_i)\, d\cx = 1 \]

What makes a kernel?

Which properties must \(K_\text{type}(\cx, \cx_i)\) have, so that the estimate \(\cph_\ch(\cx)\) is a density?

For all \(\cx\) and \(\cx_i\): \[ K_\text{type}(\cx, \cx_i) \ge 0, \qquad \int K_\text{type}(\cx, \cx_i)\, d\cx = 1 \]

\[ \int \cph_\ch(\cx)\, d\cx = \int \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} K_\text{type}(\cx, \cx_i)\, d\cx = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \int K_\text{type}(\cx, \cx_i)\, d\cx = \frac{1}{N} \cdot N = 1 \]

Usually also symmetric: \[ K_\text{type}(\cx, \cx_i) = K_\text{type}(\cx_i, \cx) \]

Assignment: \(K_\text{Gauss}(\cx, \cx_i)\) = norm.pdf(x, loc=x_i, scale=h)

Example: estimate by hand

Samples: \(\;-1,\ 0,\ 0,\ 1.5,\ 2 \quad (N = 5)\)

1. Box kernel, \(\ch = 1\): what is \(\cph_\ch(1)\)?

2. The same for \(\ch = 2\).

3. Which \(\ch\) gives a larger estimate far from all samples, e.g. at \(\cx = 5\)?

Derivation: estimate by hand

\(\ch = 1\): window \([0.5, 1.5]\), \(K = 1\) \[ \cph_1(1) = \frac{1}{5 \cdot 1} = 0.2 \]

\(\ch = 2\): window \([0, 2]\), \(K = 4\) \[ \cph_2(1) = \frac{4}{5 \cdot 2} = 0.4 \]

Gaussian kernel, \(\ch = 1\): \[ \cph_1(1) = \frac{1}{5} \sum_{i=1}^{5} \mathcal{N}(1 - \cx_i;\, 0, 1) \approx 0.226 \]

The unit test of parzen in the assignment.

Parzen windows in 2D

Ball kernel, small \(\ch\) (radius). Put it on every sample and average.

Small \(\ch\): a separate disc around every sample, gaps in between.

Ball kernel, large \(\ch\).

Large \(\ch\): the discs overlap, but the estimate is still step-like.

Gaussian kernel, small \(\ch = \sigma\).

Small \(\ch\): a separate peak around every sample.

Gaussian kernel, large \(\ch = \sigma\).

Large \(\ch\): the kernels merge into a smooth density with two clusters.

Choosing the bandwidth \(h\)

The bandwidth controls the smoothness

Can we choose \(\ch\) by maximising the likelihood of the training samples?

No: for \(\ch \to 0\) every sample gets its own spike and \(\cph_\ch(\cx_i) \to \infty\). We need unseen data.

Cross-validation

\[ \cX = \bigcup_{j=1}^{J} S_j, \quad S_i \cap S_j = \emptyset, \qquad \cX^j_{\mathrm{tst}} = S_j, \quad \cX^j_{\mathrm{trn}} = \cX \setminus S_j \]

Log-likelihood on the validation set

For every split \(j\) and bandwidth \(\ch\): \[ L^j(\ch) = \sum_{\cx \in \cX^j_{\mathrm{tst}}} \log \cph_\ch\big(\cx \,;\, \cX^j_{\mathrm{trn}}\big) \]

\[ L(\ch) = \frac{1}{J} \sum_{j=1}^{J} L^j(\ch) \] \[ \ch^* = \arg\max_{\ch} L(\ch) \]

The assignment

Assignment: Parzen windows

Part 1: estimate

parzen(x, trn_x, h)

Gaussian kernel, x can be a vector.

Part 2: choose \(\ch\)

compute_Lh(...)

10-fold cross-validation, fminbound gives \(\ch^*\) for each class.

Part 3: classify

classify_bayes_parzen(...)

Bayes with 0/1 loss, priors \(\cp(\cA) = \frac{N_\cA}{N_\cA + N_\cC}\).

parzen is evaluated for all \(\cx\) and all training samples at once: build a \(M \times N\) matrix of kernel values, then average over \(N\).

Take-away for the lab

  • Without \(\cp(\cx \mid \ck)\) we estimate it from samples, each class separately
  • In general \(\cph(\cx) = \frac{K}{N V}\): \(k\)-NN fixes \(K\), Parzen fixes \(V\)
  • Parzen: \(\cph_\ch(\cx) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_i K_\ch(\cx, \cx_i)\), the kernel is a density
  • The bandwidth \(\ch\) matters more than the kernel shape
  • Choose \(\ch\) by cross-validation: maximise the log-likelihood of unseen data
